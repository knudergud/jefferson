Professional Experience

Executive, diplomatic, and legislative communications leadership.

1801–1809 · Washington, D.C.

President of the United States

  • Authored and delivered inaugural addresses and annual messages establishing governing philosophy, public tone, and national priorities.
  • Developed unifying language for the first peaceful transfer of power between rival political parties.
  • Led executive communications during the Louisiana Purchase, westward exploration, fiscal reform, the Barbary conflict, and wartime trade restrictions.
  • Created a controlled, substantive, policy-centered executive communications model through written annual messages to Congress.
1797–1801 · Washington, D.C.

Vice President of the United States

  • Served as presiding officer of the Senate while shaping national discourse through constitutional argument and correspondence.
  • Refined arguments on civil liberties, federal power, states’ rights, and limits of executive authority.
  • Built durable messaging frameworks for an emerging national opposition movement.
1790–1793 · Washington, D.C.

Secretary of State

  • Directed diplomatic correspondence and foreign-policy communications for the first presidential administration.
  • Drafted formal communications on neutrality, commerce, constitutional authority, and the republic’s position among European powers.
  • Advised senior leadership on tone, substance, and audience implications in sensitive diplomatic matters.
1785–1789 · Paris, France

Minister to France

  • Represented the United States during major political, intellectual, and diplomatic change in Europe.
  • Produced persuasive correspondence explaining American interests to foreign officials, intellectuals, and commercial partners.
  • Built cross-cultural messaging strategies grounded in republican ideals, trade interests, and Enlightenment principles.
1775–1776; 1783–1784 · Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

Delegate, Continental Congress

  • Served as principal author of the Declaration of Independence, transforming grievances into an enduring statement of political legitimacy.
  • Drafted public documents, legislative proposals, and committee reports for national audiences during wartime.
  • Synthesized diverse political views into language capable of securing broad institutional support.
